As a Game Master, I’ve learned that Old School Renaissance (OSR) dungeons thrive on specific design philosophies that prioritize player agency and mechanical clarity over narrative hand-holding. Here are three core principles:

1. **The "Sandbox" Structure**: Avoid linear, corridor-style layouts that force players down a single path. Instead, design interconnected rooms, hidden passages, and multiple entry/exit points. This allows players to choose their own approach—whether they want to sneak, fight, or negotiate—making their decisions matter more than the DM’s script.

2. **Clarity and Consistency**: OSR games often have fewer rules than modern systems, so every rule that *does* exist must be crystal clear. If a monster has a special ability, it should be explicitly stated in its stat block. If a trap has a specific trigger, describe it precisely. Ambiguity leads to table arguments; clarity leads to smooth play.
