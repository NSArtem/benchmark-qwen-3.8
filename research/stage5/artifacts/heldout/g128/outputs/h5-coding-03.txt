To achieve **at-least-once** delivery with the **smallest possible correction**, you must decouple the *commit* of the transaction from the *delivery* of the message.

The core problem with the "mark before send" pattern is that if the process crashes after marking but before sending, the message is lost (the mark exists, but the message was never sent). Conversely, if you "send before mark" (the standard Outbox pattern), a crash after sending but before marking leads to duplicate sends (at-least-once, but not exactly-once).

### The Smallest Correction: **Transactional Outbox + Idempotent Consumer**

The smallest correction is to **revert to the standard Transactional Outbox pattern** (mark *after* send) and **enforce idempotency at the consumer**.

This is the industry-standard, minimal-change solution that guarantees **at-least-once** delivery without requiring complex distributed transactions (like 2PC) or sagas.

---

### Step-by-Step Correction

#### 1. Change the Publisher Logic: "Send Before Mark"
Instead of marking the message as "sent" before contacting the downstream service, **mark it as "pending" or "new" first**, then send, then mark as "sent".

However, the critical insight is: **Do not rely on the mark to prevent duplicates.** Instead, rely on the **consumer** to handle duplicates.

**Corrected Publisher Flow:**
1.  **Begin Transaction**
2.  **Insert** the event/message into the `outbox` table with status `PENDING`.
3.  **Commit** the transaction.
