### 1. The Ordering Invariant

The **ordering invariant** (often referred to as the **Monotonic Sequence Invariant**) states that:

> **A valid state transition must only be applied if its sequence number is strictly greater than the current `lastAppliedSequence` in the reducer’s state.**

In other words, the reducer maintains a `lastAppliedSequence` (or `lastSeq`) that tracks the highest sequence number successfully applied to the current state. Any incoming event with a sequence number **≤** `lastAppliedSequence` is considered **stale** or **duplicate** and must be **ignored** (not applied to the state).

This ensures that:
- State changes are applied in strict chronological order.
- Duplicate events do not cause double-application of side effects or state corruption.
- Out-of-order (late) events do not overwrite newer, already-applied state.

---

### 2. The Terminal-State Guard

The **terminal-state guard** (or **Terminal State Immutability**) states that:

> **Once a job reaches a terminal state (e.g., `succeeded`, `failed`, `canceled`), no subsequent events for that job may modify its state.**

Terminal states are final; they represent the end of the job’s lifecycle. Any event received after a terminal state has been applied for a given job ID must be **discarded**, regardless of its sequence number, to prevent resurrection or corruption of the completed job’s state.
