To fence a worker who claimed a job with `lease_epoch = 12` against a newer claimant with `epoch = 13`, the essential conditional UPDATE predicate must ensure that the job is only updated if the current stored epoch in the database matches the worker's claimed epoch.

### Essential Conditional UPDATE Predicate

```sql
UPDATE jobs
SET status = 'completed',
    completed_at = NOW()
WHERE id = :job_id
  AND lease_epoch = 12;
```

**Key Logic:**
- The `WHERE` clause includes `lease_epoch = 12` (the worker’s claimed epoch).
- If a newer claimant (with `lease_epoch = 13`) has already taken over the job, the database row’s `lease_epoch` will be `13`, **not** `12`.
- Therefore, the condition `lease_epoch = 12` will **fail** to match the row, and **zero rows** will be updated.

### What Zero Affected Rows Means

- **Zero affected rows** means the UPDATE statement did not modify any rows.
- In this context, it signals that **the worker’s lease has been invalidated** — a newer claimant (epoch 13) has already taken over the job.
- The worker is no longer the legitimate owner of the job and must **not** proceed to mark it as completed or perform any further actions on it.
