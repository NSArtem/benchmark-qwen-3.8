In the context of a browser-based state reducer for job processing, the system relies on **monotonic sequence numbers** to ensure consistency and prevent state corruption from out-of-order or duplicate events.

### 1. Ordering Invariant
**Invariant:** The reducer must only accept an event if its `sequence` number is **strictly greater than** the `lastProcessedSequence` currently stored in the state.

*   **Logic:** Sequence numbers represent a total order of events. If an event arrives with a sequence number less than or equal to the last processed sequence, it is considered **stale** (already processed) or **duplicate** and must be **ignored** to prevent state regression or double-processing.
*   **Exception:** If the sequence number is greater than `lastProcessedSequence` but there is a gap (i.e., `sequence > lastProcessedSequence + 1`), the event is **late** (out-of-order). The reducer must **buffer** this event and wait for the missing intermediate sequences to arrive before applying it, to maintain causal consistency.

### 2. Terminal-State Guard
**Guard:** Once the job reaches a **terminal state** (e.g., `job.succeeded` or `job.failed`), the reducer enters a **locked** or **frozen** state.

*   **Logic:** No further state transitions are permitted after a terminal state is reached. Any subsequent events (regardless of their sequence number) must be **discarded** or **ignored** to prevent the job from being "revived" or transitioning to an invalid state (e.g., a succeeded job cannot transition to `job.running`).
*   **Purpose:** This ensures that the final outcome of the job is immutable and prevents race conditions where a late-arriving non-terminal event could overwrite the final result.
